11. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation & Inverse Square Law

Module 5: Advanced Mechanics — Inquiry Question 3 (Part 1)

NESA Syllabus Alignment & Core Mechanics

Inquiry Question 3: How does the force of gravity determine the motion of celestial objects?

According to the NESA Stage 6 Physics Syllabus (calculus-free), students must master the following foundational outcomes regarding universal gravitational interactions:

  • Apply Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation to calculate the force of attraction between two point masses or uniform spherical bodies: $$F_g = \frac{G M m}{r^2}$$
  • Analyse the inverse square relationship ($F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$) conceptually and graphically, evaluating how changes in separation distance ($r$) or masses ($M, m$) scale gravitational attraction.
  • Understand the Universal Gravitational Constant ($G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$) and distinguish it clearly from local gravitational acceleration ($g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$).
  • Apply vector superposition to solve multi-body gravitational systems and locate gravitational null points (e.g. Lagrangian L1 line points where net gravitational force is zero).

Calculus-Free NESA Scope & Band 6 Distinction Insights

Point Mass & Shell Theorem Concept: Sir Isaac Newton showed that for a uniform solid sphere, the gravitational force exerted on an external object is mathematically identical to the force if all the sphere's mass were concentrated at its exact geometric center. This allows us to treat planets, moons, and stars as point masses!

Common Exam Trap ($r = R + h$): On NESA exams, questions frequently give the radius of a planet ($R$) and the altitude of a satellite above its surface ($h$). The separation distance $r$ MUST be measured from center-to-center: $r = R + h$. Forgetting to add the planet's radius is the single most frequent cause of mark loss in Module 5!

Deep Dive Part A: Newton's Law & The Inverse Square Mechanism

1. The Universal Nature of Gravitational Attraction

Before Sir Isaac Newton published his landmark Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica (1687), terrestrial gravity (falling apples) and celestial mechanics (planetary orbits) were viewed as completely separate phenomena. Newton's brilliant insight was that the exact same attractive force pulls objects to Earth as holds the Moon in its orbit.

Every particle of matter in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is:

  • Directly proportional to the product of their masses ($F_g \propto M \times m$).
  • Inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers ($F_g \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$).

Figure 1: Gravitational attraction between two spherical bodies. By Newton's Third Law, $|\vec{F}_{12}| = |\vec{F}_{21}| = \frac{G M m}{r^2}$. The forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction regardless of mass ratio!

Equation Formula Breakdown & Units

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

$$F_g = \frac{G M m}{r^2}$$

Where:

  • $F_g$: Gravitational attraction force between the two bodies ($\text{N}$).
  • $G$: Universal Gravitational Constant ($6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).
  • $M, m$: Masses of the two interacting bodies ($\text{kg}$).
  • $r$: Distance between their centers of mass ($\text{m}$).

Newton's Third Law Absolute Symmetry: A very common conceptual doubt is whether a massive planet (e.g., Earth, $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24} \text{ kg}$) exerts a larger force on an apple ($m = 0.2 \text{ kg}$) than the apple exerts on Earth. The answer is NO! By Newton's Third Law of Motion, the forces form an action-reaction pair:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{Earth on Apple}} = -\vec{F}_{\text{Apple on Earth}}$$

Both forces have the EXACT same magnitude $F_g = 1.96 \text{ N}$. However, Earth's immense mass means its resulting acceleration ($a_E = \frac{F_g}{M_E} \approx 3.28 \times 10^{-25} \text{ m/s}^2$) is unnoticeable, while the apple accelerates at $g = 9.80 \text{ m/s}^2$!

2. Geometric Origin of the Inverse Square Law ($1/r^2$)

Why does gravity follow an inverse-square relationship rather than $1/r$ or $1/r^3$? The reason is fundamentally geometric: gravity spreads out isotropically in 3D space through spherical surfaces.

Geometric Spreading Analogy

Imagine a central light bulb emitting light rays uniformly in all directions. At distance $r$, all rays pass through a sphere of surface area $A = 4\pi r^2$. At distance $2r$, the exact same number of light rays spreads over a sphere with area $4\pi (2r)^2 = 4(4\pi r^2)$, quadrupling the area. Consequently, the light intensity (or gravitational force density) drops to $\frac{1}{4}$ of its initial value!

Distance Scaling Factor ($r$) Area Scaling ($4\pi r^2$) Gravitational Force Factor ($F_g$) Example Force (Relative to $F_0 = 100 \text{ N}$)
$1 \times r_0$ (Base Distance) $1 \times A_0$ $1 \times F_0$ 100 N
$2 \times r_0$ (Doubled) $4 \times A_0$ $\frac{1}{2^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{4}} \times F_0$ 25 N
$3 \times r_0$ (Tripled) $9 \times A_0$ $\frac{1}{3^2} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{9}} \times F_0$ 11.1 N
$0.5 \times r_0$ (Halved) $0.25 \times A_0$ $\frac{1}{(1/2)^2} = \mathbf{4} \times F_0$ 400 N

Deep Dive Part B: Vector Superposition & Gravitational Null Points

1. The Principle of Superposition

Gravitational forces are additive vector quantities. When a body of mass $m$ is influenced by multiple surrounding masses ($M_1, M_2, \dots, M_n$), the net gravitational force $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$ is the vector sum of all individual gravitational forces:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \dots + \vec{F}_n = \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{G M_i m}{r_i^2} \hat{r}_i$$

2. Locating Gravitational Null Points (Lagrangian L1 Point Concept)

A **gravitational null point** is a position in space between two bodies where their opposing gravitational pulls cancel each other out completely ($\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0$).

Derivation: Null Point Distance Between Earth ($M_E$) and Moon ($M_M$)

Let $D$ be the center-to-center distance between Earth ($M_E$) and the Moon ($M_M$). Place a test mass $m$ along the collinear line between them at distance $x$ from Earth's center (so distance from Moon's center is $D - x$).

Step 1: Set Net Gravitational Force to Zero ($\Sigma F = 0$)

$$F_{\text{Earth on m}} = F_{\text{Moon on m}}$$ $$\frac{G M_E m}{x^2} = \frac{G M_M m}{(D - x)^2}$$

Step 2: Cancel Common Factors ($G$ and $m$)

$$\frac{M_E}{x^2} = \frac{M_M}{(D - x)^2}$$

Step 3: Take Square Root of Both Sides

$$\frac{\sqrt{M_E}}{x} = \frac{\sqrt{M_M}}{D - x}$$

Step 4: Solve for Null Distance $x$

$$\sqrt{M_E}(D - x) = \sqrt{M_M} x \implies \sqrt{M_E} D - \sqrt{M_E} x = \sqrt{M_M} x$$ $$\sqrt{M_E} D = (\sqrt{M_E} + \sqrt{M_M}) x$$

Collinear Gravitational Null Point Equation

$$x = D \left( \frac{\sqrt{M_E}}{\sqrt{M_E} + \sqrt{M_M}} \right) = \frac{D}{1 + \sqrt{\frac{M_M}{M_E}}}$$

Four Comprehensive Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Lab Lead Spheres Attraction Force

[Core Gravitational Law Application]

Two uniform lead spheres are placed on a laboratory bench. Sphere 1 has mass $M_1 = 15.0\text{ kg}$ and Sphere 2 has mass $M_2 = 25.0\text{ kg}$. Their centers are separated by a distance $r = 0.800\text{ m}$.

a) Calculate the gravitational force of attraction between the two lead spheres.
b) Calculate the acceleration of Sphere 1 assuming the bench is completely frictionless ($G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

Part a) Gravitational Attraction Force ($F_g$):

$$F_g = \frac{G M_1 M_2}{r^2}$$ $$F_g = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \times 15.0 \times 25.0}{(0.800)^2}$$ $$F_g = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \times 375.0}{0.640} = \frac{2.50275 \times 10^{-8}}{0.640} = \mathbf{3.91 \times 10^{-8}\text{ N}} \quad (39.1\text{ nN})$$

Part b) Acceleration of Sphere 1 ($a_1$):

By Newton's Second Law ($F = m a$):

$$a_1 = \frac{F_g}{M_1} = \frac{3.911 \times 10^{-8}}{15.0} = \mathbf{2.61 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m/s}^2}$$

Notice how extremely tiny this acceleration is! This explains why we do not feel gravitational attraction toward everyday laboratory objects.

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Astronaut Weight at High Altitude ($r = R_E + h$)

[Inverse Square Scaling & Altitude Physics]

An astronaut of mass $m = 75.0\text{ kg}$ stands on Earth's surface, where Earth's radius is $R_E = 6.371 \times 10^6\text{ m}$ and mass $M_E = 5.972 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$.

a) Calculate the astronaut's weight on Earth's surface ($F_{g,0}$).
b) The astronaut travels aboard a space station orbiting at an altitude of $h = 3 R_E$ above Earth's surface ($r = 4 R_E$). Calculate their new gravitational attraction weight $F_{g,h}$ using both proportional scaling and direct formula calculation.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Part a) Weight at Surface ($F_{g,0}$):

$$F_{g,0} = \frac{G M_E m}{R_E^2} = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5.972 \times 10^{24})(75.0)}{(6.371 \times 10^6)^2}$$ $$F_{g,0} = \frac{2.9893 \times 10^{15}}{4.05896 \times 10^{13}} = \mathbf{736.5\text{ N}} \quad (\text{matches } 75.0 \times 9.82 = 736.5\text{ N})$$

Part b) Weight at Altitude $h = 3 R_E$ ($r = R_E + 3 R_E = 4 R_E$):

Method 1: Inverse Square Proportional Scaling

Because separation distance quadruples ($r \to 4 R_E$), force scales by $\frac{1}{4^2} = \frac{1}{16}$:

$$F_{g,h} = \frac{F_{g,0}}{16} = \frac{736.47}{16} = \mathbf{46.0\text{ N}}$$

Method 2: Direct Calculation

$$r = 4 \times 6.371 \times 10^6 = 2.5484 \times 10^7\text{ m}$$ $$F_{g,h} = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5.972 \times 10^{24})(75.0)}{(2.5484 \times 10^7)^2} = \frac{2.9893 \times 10^{15}}{6.4943 \times 10^{14}} = \mathbf{46.0\text{ N}}$$

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Earth-Moon Gravitational Null Point Search

[Vector Superposition & Null Point]

The distance between the centers of Earth and the Moon is $D = 3.84 \times 10^8\text{ m}$. Earth's mass is $M_E = 5.972 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$ and the Moon's mass is $M_M = 7.348 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}$.

a) Calculate the distance $x$ from Earth's center to the point where a spacecraft experiences zero net gravitational force.
b) Express this null point location as a percentage of total Earth-Moon separation distance $D$.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Part a) Calculating Null Point Distance ($x$):

At the null point between collinear bodies:

$$\frac{G M_E m}{x^2} = \frac{G M_M m}{(D - x)^2} \implies \frac{M_E}{x^2} = \frac{M_M}{(D - x)^2}$$

Taking square root of both sides:

$$\frac{\sqrt{M_E}}{x} = \frac{\sqrt{M_M}}{D - x} \implies \sqrt{M_E}(D - x) = \sqrt{M_M} x$$

Calculating mass square roots:

$$\sqrt{M_E} = \sqrt{5.972 \times 10^{24}} = 2.44377 \times 10^{12}$$ $$\sqrt{M_M} = \sqrt{7.348 \times 10^{22}} = 2.71072 \times 10^{11}$$

Substituting into null distance formula:

$$x = D \left( \frac{\sqrt{M_E}}{\sqrt{M_E} + \sqrt{M_M}} \right)$$ $$x = (3.84 \times 10^8) \times \left( \frac{2.44377 \times 10^{12}}{2.44377 \times 10^{12} + 0.271072 \times 10^{12}} \right)$$ $$x = (3.84 \times 10^8) \times \left( \frac{2.44377}{2.71484} \right) = (3.84 \times 10^8) \times 0.90015 = \mathbf{3.46 \times 10^8\text{ m from Earth's center}}$$

Part b) Distance Percentage:

$$\% = \frac{3.4566 \times 10^8}{3.84 \times 10^8} \times 100\% = \mathbf{90.0\% \text{ of the distance to the Moon}}$$

Worked Example 4 (Extension - JRAHS Style): Force Inside Tunnel Drilled Through Uniform Earth

[Internal Gravitational Physics & Linear Scaling]

Suppose a straight narrow tunnel is drilled through the center of Earth (radius $R_E = 6.37 \times 10^6\text{ m}$, mass $M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$). A drop capsule of mass $m = 100\text{ kg}$ is released into the tunnel at internal distance $r < R_E$ from Earth's center.

a) Explain using Shell Theorem principles why only the mass enclosed within radius $r$ ($M_{\text{enclosed}}$) exerts a net gravitational force on the capsule.
b) Show that assuming uniform density $\rho$, the gravitational force inside Earth scales linearly with distance ($F_g \propto r$).
c) Calculate the force on the capsule when it is halfway to Earth's center ($r = 0.5 R_E$).

Full Band 6 JRAHS Solution:

Part a) Shell Theorem Inside Matter:

By Newton's Shell Theorem, a uniform spherical shell of matter exerts zero net gravitational force on any body located inside its interior cavity, because attraction forces from opposite sides cancel out symmetrically. Therefore, as the capsule drops to radial distance $r < R_E$, all Earth material at distances greater than $r$ exerts zero net force. Only the sphere of mass enclosed within radius $r$ pulls on the capsule!

Part b) Linear Force Scaling Derivation ($F_g \propto r$):

Earth's total mass assuming uniform density $\rho$ is $M_E = \frac{4}{3} \pi R_E^3 \rho$. The enclosed mass within radius $r$ is:

$$M_{\text{enclosed}} = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \rho = M_E \left( \frac{r^3}{R_E^3} \right)$$

Substituting $M_{\text{enclosed}}$ into Newton's Law of Gravitation:

$$F_g(r) = \frac{G M_{\text{enclosed}} m}{r^2} = \frac{G \left[ M_E \left( \frac{r^3}{R_E^3} \right) \right] m}{r^2} = \mathbf{\left( \frac{G M_E m}{R_E^3} \right) r}$$

Since $\frac{G M_E m}{R_E^3}$ is constant, $F_g(r) \propto r$. Gravity inside a uniform Earth behaves like a Hooke's Law spring ($F = -k r$), driving Simple Harmonic Motion!

Part c) Numerical Force at $r = 0.5 R_E$:

At Earth's surface ($r = R_E$), $F_{g,0} = m g = 100 \times 9.80 = 980\text{ N}$.

Since force scales linearly with distance ($r = 0.5 R_E$):

$$F_g(0.5 R_E) = 0.5 \times F_{g,0} = 0.5 \times 980 = \mathbf{490\text{ N}}$$

Practise Question 1 (Easy): Inverse Square Scaling Ratio Calculation

[3 Marks]

Two artificial research satellites experience a mutual gravitational attraction force $F_0 = 4.80 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N}$ when separated by distance $r_0$.

Calculate the new gravitational force $F_{\text{new}}$ if the separation distance is tripled ($r_{\text{new}} = 3 r_0$) AND the mass of one satellite is doubled ($m_{\text{new}} = 2 m_0$).

Hint: Force scales as $F_{\text{new}} = F_0 \times \frac{\text{Mass Factor}}{(\text{Distance Factor})^2} = F_0 \times \frac{2}{3^2} = \frac{2}{9} F_0$.

Applying proportional scaling to Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation ($F \propto \frac{m}{r^2}$):

$$F_{\text{new}} = F_0 \times \left( \frac{2}{3^2} \right) = F_0 \times \left( \frac{2}{9} \right)$$ $$F_{\text{new}} = (4.80 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N}) \times \frac{2}{9} = \mathbf{1.07 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N}} \quad (1.07\text{ \mu N})$$
Exemplar Full-Mark Written Response:

"Newton's Law of Gravitation states $F \propto \frac{M m}{r^2}$. Doubling mass multiplies force by $2$, while tripling separation distance divides force by $3^2 = 9$. Thus $F_{\text{new}} = \frac{2}{9} F_0 = \frac{2}{9} (4.80 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N}) = 1.07 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N}$."

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Identifies that doubling mass multiplies force by 2.
  • 2 Marks: Identifies that tripling distance divides force by $3^2 = 9$.
  • 3 Marks: Obtains final force $1.07 \times 10^{-6}\text{ N}$ with correct scientific notation.

Practise Question 2 (Medium): Net Force on Spacecraft Between Earth and Moon

[4 Marks]

A $2500\text{ kg}$ robotic space probe is located on the direct line between Earth and the Moon, at a distance $r_E = 3.00 \times 10^8\text{ m}$ from Earth's center. The total Earth-Moon separation distance is $D = 3.84 \times 10^8\text{ m}$. ($M_E = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}, M_M = 7.35 \times 10^{22}\text{ kg}, G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).

Calculate the magnitude and direction of the net gravitational force $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$ acting on the probe.

Hint: Probe distance from Moon is $r_M = 3.84 \times 10^8 - 3.00 \times 10^8 = 8.40 \times 10^7\text{ m}$. Earth pulls left, Moon pulls right: $F_{\text{net}} = F_E - F_M$.

Step 1: Calculate Earth's gravitational pull on probe ($F_E$):

$$F_E = \frac{G M_E m}{r_E^2} = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})(2500)}{(3.00 \times 10^8)^2} = \frac{9.9609 \times 10^{17}}{9.00 \times 10^{16}} = \mathbf{11.07\text{ N (Towards Earth)}}$$

Step 2: Calculate Moon's gravitational pull on probe ($F_M$):

Distance from Moon's center $r_M = 3.84 \times 10^8 - 3.00 \times 10^8 = 8.40 \times 10^7\text{ m}$.

$$F_M = \frac{G M_M m}{r_M^2} = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(7.35 \times 10^{22})(2500)}{(8.40 \times 10^7)^2} = \frac{1.2263 \times 10^{16}}{7.056 \times 10^{15}} = \mathbf{1.74\text{ N (Towards Moon)}}$$

Step 3: Calculate Net Gravitational Force ($\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$):

$$F_{\text{net}} = F_E - F_M = 11.07 - 1.74 = \mathbf{9.33\text{ N directed TOWARDS EARTH}}$$
Exemplar Full-Mark Written Response:

"Earth's pull is $F_E = \frac{G M_E m}{r_E^2} = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5.97 \times 10^{24})(2500)}{(3.00 \times 10^8)^2} = 11.07\text{ N}$ towards Earth. Moon distance is $r_M = 3.84 \times 10^8 - 3.00 \times 10^8 = 8.40 \times 10^7\text{ m}$, giving Moon pull $F_M = \frac{G M_M m}{r_M^2} = 1.74\text{ N}$ towards Moon. Subtracting opposing forces yields net force $F_{\text{net}} = 11.07 - 1.74 = 9.33\text{ N}$ directed towards Earth."

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Calculates Earth's gravitational force $11.07\text{ N}$.
  • 2 Marks: Determines probe distance from Moon $r_M = 8.40 \times 10^7\text{ m}$.
  • 3 Marks: Calculates Moon's gravitational force $1.74\text{ N}$.
  • 4 Marks: Subtracts opposing vectors to obtain net force $9.33\text{ N}$ directed towards Earth.

Practise Question 3 (Hard - Distinction Separator): Sun-Earth Gravitational Null Point

[5 Marks]

The Sun has mass $M_S = 1.989 \times 10^{30}\text{ kg}$ and Earth has mass $M_E = 5.972 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}$. The average Sun-Earth separation distance is $D = 1.496 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$ (1 Astronomical Unit, AU).

a) Calculate the distance $x$ from Earth's center along the Sun-Earth line to the gravitational null point (L1 Lagrange point approximation).
b) Express this distance as a percentage of 1 AU and explain its significance for solar observation satellites (like SOHO or James Webb Space Telescope).

Hint: Use $x_{\text{Earth}} = D \left( \frac{\sqrt{M_E}}{\sqrt{M_S} + \sqrt{M_E}} \right)$. Mass ratio $\frac{M_S}{M_E} \approx 333,000$.

a) Null Point Distance from Earth ($x_{\text{Earth}}$):

$$\frac{G M_S m}{(D - x)^2} = \frac{G M_E m}{x^2} \implies \frac{D - x}{x} = \sqrt{\frac{M_S}{M_E}}$$

Calculating square root of mass ratio:

$$\sqrt{\frac{M_S}{M_E}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.989 \times 10^{30}}{5.972 \times 10^{24}}} = \sqrt{333054} = 577.108$$ $$\frac{D - x}{x} = 577.108 \implies D - x = 577.108 x \implies D = 578.108 x$$ $$x = \frac{1.496 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}}{578.108} = \mathbf{2.587 \times 10^8\text{ m from Earth's center}} \quad (\approx 259,000\text{ km})$$

b) Distance Percentage & Astronomical Significance:

$$\% = \frac{2.587 \times 10^8}{1.496 \times 10^{11}} \times 100\% = \mathbf{0.173\% \text{ of 1 AU}}$$

Astronomical Significance: At the L1 region, the combined gravitational pulls of Sun and Earth provide the exact centripetal force needed for a satellite to orbit the Sun with a 1-year period, keeping it permanently parked between Earth and the Sun for uninterrupted solar viewing!

Exemplar Full-Mark Written Response:

"Equating gravitational forces $\frac{M_S}{(D-x)^2} = \frac{M_E}{x^2}$ gives $\frac{D-x}{x} = \sqrt{\frac{M_S}{M_E}} = \sqrt{3.33 \times 10^5} = 577.1$. Solving for distance from Earth yields $x = \frac{1.496 \times 10^{11}}{578.1} = 2.59 \times 10^8\text{ m}$ ($0.173\%$ of 1 AU). At this null region, solar satellites can maintain a halo orbit around L1 with an orbital period matching Earth's year, permitting continuous solar monitoring."

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Sets up force equilibrium equation $\frac{M_S}{(D-x)^2} = \frac{M_E}{x^2}$.
  • 2 Marks: Correctly computes mass ratio square root $\sqrt{M_S / M_E} = 577.1$.
  • 3 Marks: Solves correctly for distance from Earth $x = 2.59 \times 10^8\text{ m}$.
  • 4 Marks: Expresses distance as percentage of $1\text{ AU}$ ($0.173\%$).
  • 5 Marks: Explains physical significance of L1 point for space telescopes.

Practise Question 4 (JRAHS Extension - Band 6 Separator): 2D Triangular Three-Body Gravitational Vector Balance

[6 Marks]

Three identical spherical stars, each of mass $M = 2.00 \times 10^{30}\text{ kg}$, are located at the vertices of an equilateral triangle with side length $L = 4.00 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$ in deep space. A research probe of mass $m = 1200\text{ kg}$ is placed at the midpoint of one of the sides (Point P, between Star 1 and Star 2).

a) Calculate the net gravitational force $\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$ exerted on the probe at Point P.
b) Calculate the net gravitational force $\vec{F}_{\text{center}}$ exerted on the probe if it is moved to the exact centroid (geometric center) of the equilateral triangle ($G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).

Hint: At midpoint P, Star 1 and Star 2 pulls cancel identically! Net force comes purely from Star 3 at distance $h = L \sin(60^\circ) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} L$. At centroid, 3 vectors spaced $120^\circ$ apart cancel to zero.

Full Band 6 JRAHS Solution:

Part a) Net Force at Midpoint P ($\vec{F}_{\text{net}}$):

Probe is at midpoint between Star 1 and Star 2 ($r_1 = r_2 = L/2$).

$$\vec{F}_1 = -\vec{F}_2 \implies \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 = 0$$

Thus, the forces from Star 1 and Star 2 cancel completely! The net force is solely due to Star 3.

Distance from Star 3 to midpoint P is the height of the equilateral triangle ($h$):

$$h = L \sin(60.0^\circ) = (4.00 \times 10^{11}) \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 3.4641 \times 10^{11}\text{ m}$$

Gravitational force from Star 3 ($F_3$):

$$F_3 = \frac{G M_3 m}{h^2} = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(2.00 \times 10^{30})(1200)}{(3.4641 \times 10^{11})^2}$$ $$F_3 = \frac{1.60176 \times 10^{23}}{1.200 \times 10^{23}} = \mathbf{1.335\text{ N directed TOWARDS STAR 3}}$$

Part b) Net Force at Centroid ($\vec{F}_{\text{center}}$):

Distance from centroid to any vertex is $r_{\text{centroid}} = \frac{L}{\sqrt{3}}$.

Due to the 3-fold $120^\circ$ rotational symmetry of an equilateral triangle:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{center}} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3$$

Resolving vector components along any Cartesian axis ($120^\circ$ separation):
