Question 1 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2022 Oakhill College - Q1

Consider an object undergoing projectile motion under acceleration due to gravity as shown in the diagram below. Point $Y$ represents the apex of the trajectory.

Choose the most correct statement:

Correct Answer: (D)

Worked Solution: In ideal projectile motion (where air resistance is neglected), horizontal acceleration $a_x = 0$. Therefore, the horizontal velocity component remains constant throughout the entire flight ($u_x = v_x$). At the highest point $Y$, $v_y = 0$, but $v_x \neq 0$, so total velocity is non-zero.

Question 2 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2022 Oakhill College - Q2

An object travels in a circular path of radius $r$ at a constant speed $v$. What happens to the object's acceleration if the speed is doubled and the radius is halved?

Correct Answer: (D)

Worked Solution: Centripetal acceleration is given by $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$.
When speed is doubled ($v' = 2v$) and radius is halved ($r' = \frac{r}{2}$):
$$a_c' = \frac{(2v)^2}{(r/2)} = \frac{4v^2}{r/2} = 8 \left(\frac{v^2}{r}\right) = 8 a_c$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly applies $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$ with proportional scaling.

Question 3 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2022 Oakhill College - Q3

A $5\text{ m}$ uniform plank of mass $350\text{ kg}$ rests on the top of a building with $2\text{ m}$ extended over the edge as shown in the figure below.

How far can a $95\text{ kg}$ person venture past the edge of the building on the plank before the plank just begins to tip?

Correct Answer: (B)

Worked Solution: At the verge of tipping, the pivot point is the edge of the building.
Center of mass of uniform $5\text{ m}$ plank is at $2.5\text{ m}$ from left end ($0.5\text{ m}$ to the left of the edge).
Torque balance around edge: $\tau_{\text{clockwise}} = \tau_{\text{counter-clockwise}}$
$$m_{\text{person}} g x = m_{\text{plank}} g (0.5\text{ m})$$ $$95 \times x = 350 \times 0.5 = 175 \implies x = \frac{175}{95} \approx 1.84\text{ m} \approx 1.8\text{ m}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Solves torque equilibrium around the building edge correctly.

Question 4 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2022 Oakhill College - Q4

The planet Caprica has a mass three times that of Earth ($M_C = 3 M_E$). The acceleration due to gravity on Caprica is one-third that on the surface of Earth ($g_C = \frac{1}{3} g_E$). If Earth has a radius of $1$, what is the radius of the planet Caprica?

Correct Answer: (B)

Worked Solution: $g = \frac{GM}{r^2} \implies r = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{g}}$.
For Caprica: $r_C = \sqrt{\frac{G(3M_E)}{\frac{1}{3} g_E}} = \sqrt{9 \frac{GM_E}{g_E}} = 3 r_E = 3 \times 1 = 3$.

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly applies Newton's gravitational field strength relation.

Question 5 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2022 Oakhill College - Q5

Two satellites, Icarus and GoldenEye, are in circular orbits around Earth. Their masses are identical and their orbital radii are $R$ and $25R$, respectively. What is the ratio of their orbital periods, $T_I : T_G$?

Correct Answer: (D)

Worked Solution: According to Kepler's Third Law: $\frac{r^3}{T^2} = \text{constant} \implies T \propto r^{3/2}$.
$$\frac{T_I}{T_G} = \left(\frac{R_I}{R_G}\right)^{3/2} = \left(\frac{R}{25R}\right)^{3/2} = \left(\frac{1}{25}\right)^{3/2} = \frac{1}{125}$$ Ratio $T_I : T_G = 1 : 125$.

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly uses Kepler's Law of Periods proportionality $T \propto r^{3/2}$.

Question 7 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2023 JRAHS - Q2

A ball bearing X of mass $2m$ is projected vertically upwards with speed $u$. A ball bearing Y of mass $m$ is projected at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal with speed $2u$ at the same time. Air resistance is negligible. Both land on the same horizontal plane. Which statement is correct?

Correct Answer: (C)

Worked Solution: Initial vertical speed of X: $u_{y,X} = u$.
Initial vertical speed of Y: $u_{y,Y} = 2u \sin(30^\circ) = 2u(0.5) = u$.
Since vertical acceleration is $g$ for both, flight time $t = \frac{2u_y}{g}$ depends solely on $u_y$. Since $u_{y,X} = u_{y,Y}$, both reach the ground at the exact same time.

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Resolves initial vertical components and deduces equal flight time.

Question 8 (1 Mark)

2023 JRAHS - Q3

The relationship between orbital radius $R$ and period $T$ is $R^3 = \frac{GM T^2}{4\pi^2}$. Which graph correctly represents $R^3$ versus $T^2$ for Planet Y having double the mass of Planet X ($M_Y = 2 M_X$)?

Correct Answer: (B)

Question 9 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2023 JRAHS - Q6

A $22\text{ g}$ piece of plasticine is stuck to a horizontal ceiling fan blade $12\text{ cm}$ from the center. Settings: Low ($100\text{ rpm}$), Medium ($200\text{ rpm}$), High ($300\text{ rpm}$). Coefficient of static friction $\mu_s = 1.5$. On which settings will the plasticine remain attached?

Correct Answer: (B)

Worked Solution: Max static friction force: $f_{s,\max} = \mu_s m g = 1.5 \times 0.022 \times 9.80 = 0.3234\text{ N}$.
Required centripetal force: $F_c = m \omega^2 r$.
• Low ($100\text{ rpm} = 10.47\text{ rad/s}$): $F_c = 0.022 \times (10.47)^2 \times 0.12 = 0.289\text{ N} < 0.3234\text{ N}$ (Attached).
• Medium ($200\text{ rpm} = 20.94\text{ rad/s}$): $F_c = 0.022 \times (20.94)^2 \times 0.12 = 1.157\text{ N} > 0.3234\text{ N}$ (Slips).

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Compares maximum static friction force to centripetal force requirements.

Which graph best shows the gravitational potential energy ($U$) of an object in space versus distance $r$ from a source of gravity?

Question 14 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2024 ITE - Q5

Kepler's Law of Periods, summarized in $T^2 = k R^3$, shows the relationship between orbital period and radius of a planet orbiting a star. In which situation would a change in the constant $k$ be needed?

Correct Answer: (C)

Worked Solution: Equating centripetal force to gravitational force: $\frac{m v^2}{R} = \frac{GMm}{R^2} \implies \frac{R^3}{T^2} = \frac{GM}{4\pi^2} \implies T^2 = \left(\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}\right) R^3$. Thus, $k = \frac{4\pi^2}{GM_{\text{star}}}$. The constant $k$ depends ONLY on the mass of the central star ($M$). Changing the central star changes $M$, altering $k$.

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Recognises that Kepler's constant $k$ depends exclusively on central mass $M$.

Question 15 (1 Mark)

What is the minimum speed at which a bucket of water of mass $1.5\text{ kg}$ needs to be whirled in a vertical circle of radius $1.2\text{ m}$ without spilling at the top of the path?

Correct Answer: (A)

Worked Solution: At top of vertical circle, water doesn't fall when normal force $N = 0 \implies F_c = F_g$.

Two satellites R and S orbit the same planet. Satellite R has an orbital radius three times that of satellite S ($r_R = 3 r_S$). What is the ratio of their periods $T_R : T_S$?

Correct Answer: (C)

Worked Solution: $\frac{T_R^2}{T_S^2} = \frac{r_R^3}{r_S^3} = (3)^3 = 27 \implies \frac{T_R}{T_S} = \sqrt{27} \approx 5.196 \approx 5.20$.
Ratio $T_R : T_S = 5.20 : 1$.

A pendulum bob of mass $5.6\text{ g}$ on a $0.8\text{ m}$ light string is held horizontal and released. At the bottom of its swing, the string is severed. What is the initial horizontal velocity of the bob as it begins projectile motion?

Correct Answer: (B)

A road curve of radius $65\text{ m}$ is banked at an angle of $12^\circ$. What is the recommended design speed ($v$) for a vehicle to safely navigate this curve without relying on friction?

Question 19 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2023 Oakhill College - Task 1

In a circular motion experiment, a $0.02\text{ kg}$ rubber stopper is rotated horizontally at radius $1.2\text{ m}$ supported by a hanging mass of $0.10\text{ kg}$. What is the period ($T$) of one revolution?

Correct Answer: (A)

Worked Solution: Tension $F_c = M_{\text{hanging}} g = 0.10 \times 9.80 = 0.98\text{ N}$.
$$F_c = \frac{4\pi^2 m r}{T^2} \implies T = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi^2 m r}{F_c}} = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi^2 \times 0.02 \times 1.2}{0.98}} = \sqrt{0.967} \approx 0.983\text{ s} \approx 0.98\text{ s}$$

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Equates hanging weight tension to centripetal force to solve for period $T$.

Question 20 (1 Mark)

[1 Mark]
2024 ITE - Section I Conceptual

A space probe is launched vertically upward from Earth's surface ($R_E$) with an initial launch speed equal to half its escape velocity ($u = \frac{1}{2} v_{\text{esc}}$). Neglecting air resistance, what maximum distance $r$ from the center of Earth will the probe reach?

Correct Answer: (C)

Worked Solution: $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R_E}} \implies u^2 = \frac{1}{4} v_{\text{esc}}^2 = \frac{GM}{2 R_E}$.
Total energy $E_i = K_i + U_i = \frac{1}{2} m \left(\frac{GM}{2 R_E}\right) - \frac{GMm}{R_E} = -\frac{3 GMm}{4 R_E}$.
At maximum radial height $r$, $v = 0 \implies E_f = -\frac{GMm}{r}$.
Conservation of energy: $-\frac{GMm}{r} = -\frac{3 GMm}{4 R_E} \implies r = \frac{4}{3} R_E$.

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Applies conservation of total mechanical energy to determine maximum orbital radius $r$.
