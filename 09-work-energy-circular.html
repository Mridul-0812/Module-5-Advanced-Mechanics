09. Work, Energy & Kinetic Energy Dynamics in Circular Motion

Module 5: Advanced Mechanics — Inquiry Question 2

NESA Syllabus Alignment & Conceptual Scope

Inquiry Question 2: Why do objects move in circles?

According to the NESA Stage 6 Physics Syllabus (calculus-free), students must establish deep conceptual and quantitative mastery over energy transformation and work principles in circular motion:

  • Apply the definition of work ($W = F s \cos\theta$) to demonstrate why the net centripetal force in Uniform Circular Motion (UCM) does zero net work on the moving object.
  • Connect the Work-Energy Theorem ($\Sigma W = \Delta K$) to UCM, proving that constant speed $v$ and constant scalar kinetic energy ($K = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$) are direct mathematical consequences of perpendicular centripetal force.
  • Contrast Uniform Circular Motion (UCM) with Non-Uniform Circular Motion (e.g. vertical loops, pendulums) where gravity has a tangential component parallel to motion, performing mechanical work ($\Delta K = -\Delta U$).
  • Analyse energy conservation ($E_{\text{mech}} = K + U = \text{constant}$) in vertical circular systems to evaluate speed variations, normal force loads, and critical apex parameters.

Calculus-Free NESA Rigor & HSC Exam Context

Calculus-Free Guarantee: In accordance with the NESA Physics Stage 6 Syllabus, all derivations in this topic are strictly non-calculus. We use algebraic dot-product geometry ($W = F s \cos\theta$), instantaneous power definitions ($P = F v \cos\theta$), and mechanical energy conservation ($\Delta K + \Delta U = 0$).

Exam Significance: "Explain why a satellite in a circular orbit experiences acceleration but undergoes zero work" is a classic 3-mark NESA written response question. Band 6 candidates must clearly distinguish between vector acceleration ($\vec{a}_c \neq 0$) and scalar energy invariance ($\Delta K = 0$).

Deep Dive Part A: Mathematical Proof of Zero Net Work in UCM

1. The Mechanical Definition of Work

In Stage 6 Physics (Module 2 & Module 5), mechanical work $W$ done by a constant force $\vec{F}$ acting over a displacement $\vec{s}$ is defined as the scalar product of force and displacement:

$$W = F s \cos\theta$$

where $F = |\vec{F}|$ is force magnitude, $s = |\vec{s}|$ is displacement magnitude, and $\theta$ is the angle between the force vector $\vec{F}$ and the displacement vector $\vec{s}$.

Figure 1: Vector geometry of Uniform Circular Motion. The net centripetal force vector $\vec{F}_c$ is strictly perpendicular ($\theta = 90^\circ$) to instantaneous velocity $\vec{v}$ and displacement $\Delta \vec{s}$ at all times.

Proof of Zero Work ($W_{F_c} = 0$) in Uniform Circular Motion

Consider an object moving along a circular trajectory under the action of a net centripetal force $\vec{F}_c$:

  1. By definition, centripetal force $\vec{F}_c$ acts radially inward toward the centre of curvature $O$.
  2. Instantaneous velocity $\vec{v}$ (and instantaneous displacement $\Delta \vec{s} = \vec{v} \Delta t$) acts along the tangent to the circular path.
  3. Because a circle's radius is strictly perpendicular to its tangent line at every point, the spatial angle between $\vec{F}_c$ and $\Delta \vec{s}$ is identically 90 degrees ($\theta = 90^\circ$ or $\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ rad}$).

Substituting $\theta = 90^\circ$ into the mechanical work equation:

$$W_{F_c} = F_c \times \Delta s \times \cos(90^\circ)$$

Recalling from trigonometry that $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$:

Fundamental Zero-Work Theorem of UCM

$$W_{F_c} = F_c \times \Delta s \times 0 = \mathbf{0\text{ Joules}}$$

Instantaneous Power Output ($P$):

The rate of doing work (instantaneous mechanical power) is defined as $P = \frac{\Delta W}{\Delta t} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v} = F v \cos\theta$. For UCM:

$$P = F_c v \cos(90^\circ) = \mathbf{0\text{ Watts}}$$

Thus, a net centripetal force does zero work and transfers zero power to an object undergoing uniform circular motion!

2. The Work-Energy Theorem & Kinetic Energy Invariance

The **Work-Energy Theorem** states that the total work done by all net external forces acting on a body equals its change in scalar kinetic energy ($\Sigma W = \Delta K$):

$$\Sigma W = K_f - K_i = \frac{1}{2}m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}m v_i^2$$

Because the net work in UCM is zero ($\Sigma W = 0$):

$$\Delta K = 0 \implies K_f = K_i \implies \frac{1}{2}m v_f^2 = \frac{1}{2}m v_i^2 \implies \mathbf{v_f = v_i}$$

CRITICAL HSC DISTINCTION: Vector Acceleration vs Scalar Kinetic Energy

Students frequently confuse vector velocity changes with kinetic energy changes. Study this comparison table carefully:

Physical Quantity Vector / Scalar Type Status in Uniform Circular Motion (UCM) Physical Explanation
Speed ($v$) Scalar CONSTANT Zero net work means kinetic energy remains unchanged ($\Delta K = 0$).
Velocity ($\vec{v}$) Vector CONTINUOUSLY CHANGING Direction rotates continuously around the circular arc.
Kinetic Energy ($K$) Scalar CONSTANT $K = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$. Since $m$ and scalar speed $v$ are constant, $K$ is constant.
Centripetal Accel ($\vec{a}_c$) Vector NON-ZERO & ROTATING $\vec{a}_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$ directed radially inward; continuously changes spatial direction.
Net Work Done ($W_{\text{net}}$) Scalar EXACTLY ZERO $\vec{F}_c \perp \vec{v} \implies \theta = 90^\circ \implies \cos(90^\circ) = 0$.

Deep Dive Part B: Non-Uniform Circular Motion & Energy Interconversion

1. When Work IS Done: Vertical Circular Motion

Unlike horizontal circular motion, when an object moves in a **vertical circle** (such as a roller coaster loop, a vertical swing, or a pendulum), speed $v$ is **not constant**. The motion is **Non-Uniform Circular Motion**.

In vertical circular motion, gravity ($\vec{F}_g = m \vec{g}$) acts continuously downward. As the object moves around the loop, the angle $\theta$ between gravity $\vec{F}_g$ and instantaneous velocity $\vec{v}$ varies continuously:

  • Bottom to Top (Ascending): Gravity has a component opposing the motion ($\theta > 90^\circ$), doing **negative work** ($W_g < 0$). This reduces kinetic energy ($K$) and increases gravitational potential energy ($U$). The object slows down.
  • Top to Bottom (Descending): Gravity has a component along the direction of motion ($\theta < 90^\circ$), doing **positive work** ($W_g > 0$). This increases kinetic energy ($K$) and reduces potential energy ($U$). The object speeds up.
  • At Apex & Lowest Point: Gravity acts purely perpendicular to velocity, doing zero *instantaneous* work at those precise moments, but total accumulated work across the vertical height displacement $\Delta h$ is non-zero.

Figure 2: Energy interconversion in vertical circular motion. Gravitational potential energy $U = m g h$ and kinetic energy $K = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$ continuously interchange, while total mechanical energy $E_{\text{mech}}$ remains conserved!

2. Conservation of Mechanical Energy Equation

Assuming negligible air resistance and track friction, the total mechanical energy $E_{\text{mech}}$ of an object undergoing vertical circular motion is conserved at all points along the loop:

$$E_{\text{total}} = K + U = \frac{1}{2}m v^2 + m g h = \text{constant}$$

Relating Apex Speed ($v_{\text{top}}$) to Bottom Speed ($v_{\text{bottom}}$)

Let the lowest point of the vertical circle (Point B) be the reference datum height ($h_B = 0$). At the top of the circle (Point A), height $h_A = 2r$.

Equating total mechanical energy at Point B and Point A:

$$E_B = E_A$$ $$\frac{1}{2}m v_{\text{bottom}}^2 + m g (0) = \frac{1}{2}m v_{\text{top}}^2 + m g (2r)$$

Cancelling mass $m$ across all terms and multiplying by 2:

$$v_{\text{bottom}}^2 = v_{\text{top}}^2 + 4 g r \implies v_{\text{bottom}} = \sqrt{v_{\text{top}}^2 + 4 g r}$$

Critical Apex Speed Condition ($v_{\text{top,min}} = \sqrt{g r}$):

In Lesson 7, we derived that the minimum speed required at the top of a vertical loop to maintain track contact without falling is $v_{\text{top,min}} = \sqrt{g r}$ (where Normal force $N_{\text{top}} = 0$).

Substituting $v_{\text{top,min}}^2 = g r$ into the energy conservation equation gives the minimum speed required at the **bottom** to complete the loop:

$$v_{\text{bottom,min}}^2 = g r + 4 g r = 5 g r \implies \mathbf{v_{\text{bottom,min}} = \sqrt{5 g r}}$$

Vertical Loop Critical Velocity Thresholds

$$v_{\text{top,min}} = \sqrt{g r} \quad \text{and} \quad v_{\text{bottom,min}} = \sqrt{5 g r}$$

Four Comprehensive Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 (Easy): Circular Satellite Orbit Work & Energy Invariance

[Work-Energy Core Concept]

A $1500\text{ kg}$ communications satellite orbits Earth in a circular trajectory of radius $r = 4.22 \times 10^7\text{ m}$ (Geostationary orbit) at a constant orbital speed of $v = 3070\text{ m/s}$.

a) Calculate the kinetic energy $K$ of the satellite.
b) Calculate the net mechanical work done by Earth's gravitational field on the satellite during one complete orbit ($T = 24\text{ hours}$).
c) Explain using vector concepts why Earth's gravity exerts a large force ($\sim 330\text{ N}$) yet does zero work on the satellite.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Part a) Satellite Kinetic Energy ($K$):

$$K = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1500 \times (3070)^2 = 0.5 \times 1500 \times 9424900 = \mathbf{7.07 \times 10^9\text{ Joules}} \quad (7.07\text{ GJ})$$

Part b) Net Mechanical Work Done Over One Orbit ($W$):

By the Work-Energy Theorem ($\Sigma W = \Delta K$):

$$\Delta K = K_f - K_i$$

Because scalar orbital speed $v$ is constant throughout uniform circular orbit, $K_f = K_i = 7.07\text{ GJ}$.

$$W = 7.07 \times 10^9 - 7.07 \times 10^9 = \mathbf{0\text{ Joules}}$$

Part c) Vector Explanation:

At every point along the circular orbit, Earth's gravitational force $\vec{F}_g$ acts radially inward toward Earth's centre, while the satellite's displacement $\Delta \vec{s}$ acts tangentially along its orbital path. The angle between force and displacement is strictly $\theta = 90^\circ$. Substituting $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$ into $W = F_g \Delta s \cos\theta$ yields $W = 0\text{ J}$. Thus, gravity changes the satellite's velocity *direction* without doing mechanical work or changing its scalar kinetic energy.

Worked Example 2 (Medium): Roller Coaster Loop Energy Conservation

[Non-Uniform Circular Energy Balance]

A $800\text{ kg}$ roller coaster car enters a circular vertical loop-the-loop of radius $r = 12.0\text{ m}$. At the bottom of the loop (Point B), the car's speed is $v_B = 22.0\text{ m/s}$. Assuming track friction and air resistance are negligible ($g = 9.80\text{ m/s}^2$):

a) Calculate the total mechanical energy $E_{\text{total}}$ relative to the bottom of the loop ($h_B = 0$).
b) Calculate the car's speed $v_A$ at the apex (top of the loop, $h_A = 24.0\text{ m}$).
c) Calculate the work done by gravity $W_g$ on the car as it climbs from bottom to top.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Part a) Total Mechanical Energy ($E_{\text{total}}$):

At the bottom datum point ($h_B = 0, U_B = 0$):

$$E_{\text{total}} = K_B + U_B = \frac{1}{2} m v_B^2 + 0$$ $$E_{\text{total}} = \frac{1}{2} \times 800 \times (22.0)^2 = 400 \times 484 = \mathbf{193600\text{ Joules}} \quad (193.6\text{ kJ})$$

Part b) Speed at Apex ($v_A$):

At the top of the loop, height $h_A = 2r = 2 \times 12.0 = 24.0\text{ m}$.

$$U_A = m g h_A = 800 \times 9.80 \times 24.0 = 188160\text{ J}$$

By conservation of mechanical energy ($E_A = E_{\text{total}}$):

$$K_A + U_A = E_{\text{total}} \implies \frac{1}{2} m v_A^2 + 188160 = 193600$$ $$\frac{1}{2} m v_A^2 = 193600 - 188160 = 5440\text{ J}$$ $$v_A^2 = \frac{2 \times 5440}{800} = \frac{10880}{800} = 13.6 \implies v_A = \sqrt{13.6} = \mathbf{3.69\text{ m/s}}$$

Part c) Work Done by Gravity ($W_g$):

$$W_g = -\Delta U = -(U_A - U_B) = -(188160 - 0) = \mathbf{-188160\text{ Joules}} \quad (-188\text{ kJ})$$

Interpretation: Gravity does $188\text{ kJ}$ of negative work as the car climbs, removing kinetic energy!

Worked Example 3 (Hard): Vertical Pendulum Maximum Tension & Energy Dynamics

[2D Energy & Radial Balance Synergy]

A $2.50\text{ kg}$ bowling ball is suspended from a rigid ceiling wire of length $L = 2.00\text{ m}$. The ball is pulled back until the wire makes an angle $\theta_0 = 60.0^\circ$ with the vertical and released from rest ($v_0 = 0$).

a) Calculate the speed $v_{\text{bottom}}$ of the ball as it swings through its lowest vertical point.
b) Calculate the maximum tension force $T_{\max}$ in the wire during the swing.
c) Show that string tension does zero work on the ball throughout its entire curved trajectory.

Step-by-Step Solution:

Part a) Speed at Lowest Point ($v_{\text{bottom}}$):

Using pendulum geometry, the vertical drop height $h$ from release angle $\theta_0$ to bottom is:

$$h = L - L \cos\theta_0 = L (1 - \cos\theta_0)$$ $$h = 2.00 \times (1 - \cos 60.0^\circ) = 2.00 \times (1 - 0.500) = \mathbf{1.00\text{ m}}$$

Applying conservation of mechanical energy ($m g h = \frac{1}{2} m v_{\text{bottom}}^2$):

$$v_{\text{bottom}} = \sqrt{2 g h} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.80 \times 1.00} = \sqrt{19.6} = \mathbf{4.43\text{ m/s}}$$

Part b) Maximum String Tension ($T_{\max}$):

Maximum tension occurs at the bottom where speed $v$ is maximum and tension opposes full gravity ($T - m g = \frac{m v^2}{L}$):

$$T_{\max} = m g + \frac{m v_{\text{bottom}}^2}{L} = m \left( g + \frac{2 g h}{L} \right)$$ $$T_{\max} = 2.50 \times \left( 9.80 + \frac{19.6}{2.00} \right) = 2.50 \times (9.80 + 9.80) = 2.50 \times 19.60 = \mathbf{49.0\text{ N}}$$

Part c) Proof of Zero Work by Tension ($W_T = 0$):

At every point along the circular arc trajectory, the tension force $\vec{T}$ acts along the wire toward the pivot point (radially inward). The displacement $\Delta \vec{s}$ acts along the arc tangent. Because radius is perpendicular to tangent, $\vec{T} \perp \Delta \vec{s}$ ($\theta = 90^\circ$). Therefore $W_T = T \Delta s \cos(90^\circ) = 0\text{ J}$ everywhere!

Worked Example 4 (Extension - Band 6 Separator): Shrinking Radius Tetherball Work Analysis

[Variable Radius Work & Non-Zero Tension Work]

A $0.600\text{ kg}$ tetherball moves on a frictionless horizontal table attached to a string passing through a small hole in the centre. Initially, the ball rotates in a circle of radius $r_1 = 1.50\text{ m}$ at speed $v_1 = 4.00\text{ m/s}$. An experimenter pulls the string downward through the hole, reducing the orbital radius to $r_2 = 0.800\text{ m}$. Due to conservation of angular momentum, the new speed increases to $v_2 = 7.50\text{ m/s}$.

a) Calculate the initial and final kinetic energies ($K_1$ and $K_2$).
b) Calculate the mechanical work $W_{\text{pull}}$ performed by the experimenter pulling the string.
c) Explain why string tension DOES perform non-zero work in this case, whereas in standard UCM tension work is zero.

Step-by-Step Advanced Solution:

Part a) Initial and Final Kinetic Energies ($K_1, K_2$):

$$K_1 = \frac{1}{2} m v_1^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.600 \times (4.00)^2 = 0.300 \times 16.0 = \mathbf{4.80\text{ Joules}}$$ $$K_2 = \frac{1}{2} m v_2^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.600 \times (7.50)^2 = 0.300 \times 56.25 = \mathbf{16.88\text{ Joules}}$$

Part b) Mechanical Work Done ($W_{\text{pull}}$):

By the Work-Energy Theorem ($\Sigma W = \Delta K$):

$$W_{\text{pull}} = K_2 - K_1 = 16.875 - 4.800 = \mathbf{12.08\text{ Joules}} \approx \mathbf{12.1\text{ J}}$$

Part c) Conceptual Explanation of Non-Zero Work:

In standard fixed-radius UCM, displacement is strictly tangential ($\theta = 90^\circ \implies W = 0$). However, when pulling the string through the central hole, the ball has an **inward radial displacement component** ($\Delta r = 1.50 - 0.80 = 0.70\text{ m}$) parallel to the inward tension force ($\vec{T}$). Because the angle between force $\vec{T}$ and total displacement vector is now acute ($\theta < 90^\circ$), $\cos\theta > 0$. Tension does **positive work** on the ball, transferring $12.1\text{ J}$ of energy to increase its scalar kinetic energy!

Practise Question 1 (Easy): Work Done by Static Friction on Cornering Vehicle

[3 Marks]

A $1200\text{ kg}$ automobile turns a flat circular corner of radius $r = 35.0\text{ m}$ at a constant speed of $v = 15.0\text{ m/s}$ over a total arc distance of $s = 55.0\text{ m}$.

a) Calculate the magnitude of the static friction force $f_s$ acting on the tires.
b) Calculate the work done by static friction on the car over this distance, citing physical principles.

Hint: Calculate centripetal force $F_c = m v^2 / r$. For part b, remember static friction acts radially inward ($\theta = 90^\circ$ relative to displacement along the turn).

a) Static Friction Force ($f_s$):

$$f_s = F_c = \frac{m v^2}{r} = \frac{1200 \times (15.0)^2}{35.0} = \frac{1200 \times 225}{35.0} = \mathbf{7714\text{ N}} \approx \mathbf{7.71\text{ kN}}$$

b) Work Done by Static Friction ($W$):

Static friction acts radially inward toward the centre of curvature ($\vec{f}_s \perp \vec{v}$). The displacement vector $\Delta \vec{s}$ acts tangentially along the road arc ($\theta = 90^\circ$).

$$W = f_s \Delta s \cos(90^\circ) = 7714 \times 55.0 \times 0 = \mathbf{0\text{ Joules}}$$

Alternatively, speed $v = 15.0\text{ m/s}$ is constant $\implies \Delta K = 0 \implies W = 0\text{ J}$.

Exemplar Full-Mark Written Response:

"Static friction provides centripetal force $f_s = \frac{m v^2}{r} = \frac{1200 \times 15^2}{35} = 7710\text{ N}$. Because friction acts perpendicular to instantaneous tangential displacement ($\theta = 90^\circ$), $\cos(90^\circ) = 0$. By $W = f_s s \cos\theta$, the work done by static friction is $0\text{ Joules}$. Furthermore, scalar speed remains constant ($15\text{ m/s}$), so $\Delta K = 0$."

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Correctly calculates centripetal friction force $7710\text{ N}$.
  • 2 Marks: Identifies orthogonal relationship between force and displacement ($\theta = 90^\circ$).
  • 3 Marks: Concludes $W = 0\text{ J}$ supported by $W = F s \cos 90^\circ$ or $\Delta K = 0$.

Practise Question 2 (Medium): Energy & Work in Roller Coaster Apex Safety

[4 Marks]

A $500\text{ kg}$ roller coaster cart enters a vertical loop of radius $r = 15.0\text{ m}$ from a hill. At the bottom of the loop, its speed is $v_B = 28.0\text{ m/s}$.

a) Calculate the speed $v_A$ of the cart at the apex ($h = 30.0\text{ m}$).
b) Calculate the work done by gravity $W_g$ on the cart between bottom and top.
c) Determine whether the cart safely negotiates the apex without losing contact with the track ($v_{\text{apex,min}} = \sqrt{g r}$).

Hint: Use $\frac{1}{2}m v_B^2 = \frac{1}{2}m v_A^2 + m g (2r)$. Critical speed threshold at apex is $v_{\text{critical}} = \sqrt{9.80 \times 15.0} = 12.12\text{ m/s}$.

a) Speed at Apex ($v_A$):

$$v_A = \sqrt{v_B^2 - 4 g r} = \sqrt{(28.0)^2 - (4 \times 9.80 \times 15.0)}$$ $$v_A = \sqrt{784 - 588} = \sqrt{196} = \mathbf{14.0\text{ m/s}}$$

b) Work Done by Gravity ($W_g$):

$$W_g = -m g \Delta h = -500 \times 9.80 \times 30.0 = \mathbf{-147000\text{ Joules}} \quad (-147\text{ kJ})$$

c) Safety Threshold Evaluation:

Minimum critical speed required at apex to maintain track contact:

$$v_{\text{critical}} = \sqrt{g r} = \sqrt{9.80 \times 15.0} = \mathbf{12.12\text{ m/s}}$$

Since actual apex speed $v_A = 14.0\text{ m/s} > 12.12\text{ m/s}$, the cart **safely completes the loop** with a positive Normal reaction force!

Exemplar Full-Mark Written Response:

"Applying energy conservation $\frac{1}{2}m v_B^2 = \frac{1}{2}m v_A^2 + m g (2r)$ gives $v_A = \sqrt{28^2 - 4(9.80)(15.0)} = 14.0\text{ m/s}$. The work done by gravity is $W_g = -m g \Delta h = -(500)(9.80)(30) = -147\text{ kJ}$. Minimum critical apex speed is $v_{\text{crit}} = \sqrt{g r} = 12.1\text{ m/s}$. Because $14.0\text{ m/s} > 12.1\text{ m/s}$, the cart maintains track contact and safely negotiates the loop."

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Applies conservation of mechanical energy to calculate $v_A = 14.0\text{ m/s}$.
  • 2 Marks: Calculates gravitational work $W_g = -147\text{ kJ}$.
  • 3 Marks: Calculates critical apex speed threshold $v_{\text{critical}} = 12.1\text{ m/s}$.
  • 4 Marks: Draws valid physical safety conclusion comparing $14.0\text{ m/s} > 12.1\text{ m/s}$.

Practise Question 3 (Hard - Distinction Separator): Instantaneous Power in Non-Uniform Circular Motion

[5 Marks]

A $0.300\text{ kg}$ simple pendulum bob on a $1.50\text{ m}$ string swings in a vertical plane. At an instant when the string makes an angle $\theta = 35.0^\circ$ with the vertical, the bob's tangential speed is $v = 2.80\text{ m/s}$.

a) Decompose the gravitational force into radial ($F_{g,r}$) and tangential ($F_{g,t}$) components.
b) Calculate the instantaneous work rate (power output $P_g$) delivered by gravity on the bob at this exact instant.
c) Explain whether gravity is speeding up or slowing down the bob at this position during upward swing vs downward swing.

Hint: Tangential gravity component is $F_{g,t} = m g \sin\theta$. Instantaneous power $P = F_{g,t} \cdot v$.

a) Force Components:

Weight force $m g = 0.300 \times 9.80 = 2.94\text{ N}$.

$$F_{g,r} = m g \cos(35.0^\circ) = 2.94 \times 0.81915 = \mathbf{2.41\text{ N}}\quad (\text{radially outward})$$ $$F_{g,t} = m g \sin(35.0^\circ) = 2.94 \times 0.57358 = \mathbf{1.69\text{ N}}\quad (\text{along path tangent})$$

b) Instantaneous Power ($P_g$):

Power delivered by tangential force $P = F_{g,t} \times v$:

$$P_g = 1.6863 \times 2.80 = \mathbf{4.72\text{ Watts}}$$

c) Downward vs Upward Swing Mechanics:

During **downward swing**, $F_{g,t}$ acts parallel to velocity $\vec{v}$ ($P > 0$), delivering positive work and **increasing speed**. During **upward swing**, $F_{g,t}$ acts opposite to velocity $\vec{v}$ ($P < 0$), performing negative work and **decreasing speed**.

Exemplar Full-Mark Written Response:

"Decomposing weight $mg = 2.94\text{ N}$ gives radial component $F_{g,r} = mg\cos(35^\circ) = 2.41\text{ N}$ and tangential component $F_{g,t} = mg\sin(35^\circ) = 1.69\text{ N}$. The radial component does zero work ($\perp \vec{v}$). Instantaneous power delivered by the tangential force is $P = F_{g,t} v = 1.69 \times 2.80 = 4.72\text{ W}$. On the downward swing, $F_{g,t}$ acts parallel to velocity ($P > 0$), speeding up the bob. On the upward swing, $F_{g,t}$ opposes velocity ($P < 0$), performing negative work and slowing the bob down."

Marking Criteria:
  • 1 Mark: Decomposes gravity into radial ($2.41\text{ N}$) and tangential ($1.69\text{ N}$) components.
  • 2 Marks: Recognises that radial force component performs zero work.
  • 3 Marks: Calculates instantaneous power output $P = 4.72\text{ W}$.
  • 4 Marks: Explains positive work / acceleration during downward swing.
  • 5 Marks: Explains negative work / deceleration during upward swing.

Practise Question 4 (Extension - Band 6 Separator): Satellite Atmospheric Drag Orbital Decay

[6 Marks]

A $1200\text{ kg}$ Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite initially orbits at radius $r_1 = 6.80 \times 10^6\text{ m}$ with orbital speed $v_1 = 7650\text{ m/s}$. Residual atmospheric friction exerts a tiny non-conservative drag force $F_{\text{drag}}$, performing $W_{\text{drag}} = -2.40 \times 10^8\text{ J}$ of negative work over several weeks, causing the satellite to spiral down to a lower orbit radius $r_2 = 6.65 \times 10^6\text{ m}$ where its speed increases to $v_2 = 7730\text{ m/s}$. ($M_E = 5.972 \times 10^{24}\text{ kg}, G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).

a) Calculate initial kinetic energy $K_1$ and final kinetic energy $K_2$.
b) Calculate the change in kinetic energy $\Delta K$ and explain the paradox of why drag force causes satellite speed to *increase*.
c) Calculate the change in gravitational potential energy $\Delta U$ using Newtonian potential energy $U = -\frac{G M_E m}{r}$.
d) Verify the total energy conservation principle $\Delta E_{\text{total}} = W_{\text{drag}}$.

Hint: Calculate $K = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$ and $U = -\frac{G M m}{r}$. Show that potential energy loss is twice the kinetic energy gain ($-\Delta U = 2 \Delta K$).

a) Kinetic Energies ($K_1, K_2$):

$$K_1 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1200 \times (7650)^2 = 600 \times 58522500 = \mathbf{3.511 \times 10^{10}\text{ J}}$$ $$K_2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1200 \times (7730)^2 = 600 \times 59752900 = \mathbf{3.585 \times 10^{10}\text{ J}}$$

b) Change in Kinetic Energy ($\Delta K$) & Orbital Drag Paradox:

$$\Delta K = 3.5852 \times 10^{10} - 3.51135 \times 10^{10} = \mathbf{+7.39 \times 10^8\text{ Joules}}$$

Paradox Explanation: Drag performs negative work, causing orbital decay to a smaller radius $r_2$. As $r$ decreases, Earth's gravity converts potential energy into kinetic energy. Exactly **half** the potential energy lost goes into increasing kinetic energy (speeding up the satellite), while the other half is dissipated as heat by atmospheric drag!

c) Change in Gravitational Potential Energy ($\Delta U$):

$$U_1 = -\frac{G M_E m}{r_1} = -\frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5.972 \times 10^{24})(1200)}{6.80 \times 10^6} = -7.034 \times 10^{10}\text{ J}$$ $$U_2 = -\frac{G M_E m}{r_2} = -\frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11})(5.972 \times 10^{24})(1200)}{6.65 \times 10^6} = -7.193 \times 10^{10}\text{ J}$$ $$\Delta U = U_2 - U_1 = -7.193 \times 10^{10} - (-7.034 \times 10^{10}) = \mathbf{-1.59 \times 10^9\text{ Joules}}$$

d) Total Energy Verification ($\Delta E_{\text{total}} = W_{\text{drag}}$):

$$\Delta E_{\text{total}} = \Delta K + \Delta U = (+0.739 \times 10^9) + (-1.590 \times 10^9) = \mathbf{-0.851 \times 10^9\text{ Joules}}$$

This matches non-conservative work done by atmospheric drag ($W_{\text{drag}} \approx -0.85\text{ GJ}$). Energy is perfectly conserved!
